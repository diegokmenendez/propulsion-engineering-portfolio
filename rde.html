X Energy Lab - 2 in Rotating Detonation Engine

I designed and built a 2 in rotating detonation engine and took it from an empty room to its first hotfire. I owned the design, fabrication, testing, and integration of every component, from the engine itself to the facility around it. Since first hotfire, I have conducted hundreds more, sustaining detonation for up to 2.2 seconds across a range of operating conditions. These experiments have characterized the response and failure mechanisms of copper, tungsten, and Inconel 625 and supported one presentation and three accepted conference abstracts.

Initial Design

In February 2025, I was tasked with adapting an Air Force Research Lab rotating detonation engine design for our lab. I scaled the engine down to a 2 in design, including the injector, manifolding, and feed interfaces, and redesigned the sealing mechanism to suit our facility. I performed calculations to ensure that the engine would still operate with sustained detonation.

Exploded view: ignitor ring, innerbody testing coupon, outerbody, injector rings, manifold

Facility Design

We began with no facility capable of hotfiring an engine. I designed the facility from scratch: the feed system, data acquisition, igniter system, engine control system, and the in-room gas supply, along with every supporting component. I then built each component, tested it, and integrated it into the full system.

This feed system can supply gaseous methane and oxygen at a total mass flow rate of up to 600 g/s at an equivalence ratio of 1.15. The flow duration, mass flow rate, and equivalence ratio are customizable for each hotfire.

RDE test facility

First Hotfire

It took 10 months from the start of the design in February 2025 to our first hotfire in December 2025. Before firing, I ran a nitrogen flow testing campaign on the engine to validate the feed system and flow behavior. That campaign led to our first hotfire and a successful detonation.

Collecting Data

We have since expanded our diagnostics to include laser Doppler vibrometry, high-speed imaging, and chamber pressure measurement. Next, we plan to add direct optical imaging of the detonation through a quartz outer body.

1D Thermal Modeling

I created a simplified 1D thermal model to assess how we can expect different materials to fail. This model was then calibrated to real testing data, and it gives an insight into the chamber conditions that we would not otherwise have.

Presenting Our Work

The focus of this research has been investigating how materials respond and fail in the extreme thermomechanical loading environment of a rotating detonation engine. The future plan is to design new materials that can withstand that environment.

This work has been presented once, as a first-author oral presentation at IWDP 2026. Three further papers have accepted abstracts for AIAA SciTech Forum 2027: one first-author and two co-authored. Links below.
